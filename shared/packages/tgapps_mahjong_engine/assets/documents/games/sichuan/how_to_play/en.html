How to Play Sichuan Mahjong

No chows, no honors, and one suit must go.

The Goal

Complete four sets (runs, pungs or kongs) and a pair, or seven pairs. Sichuan uses only the 108 suit tiles: no honors, no flowers and no chows. A run of three in one suit counts only when you draw its tiles yourself: a discard can't be claimed for one.

The Void Suit

Before the first discard, each player chooses one suit to give up. You must discard every tile of that suit before any other, you cannot claim it, and you cannot win while you still hold one.

Playing

  • Draw a tile and discard one. Tap a tile once to lift it and again to discard.
  • Claim another player's discard as a Pung or Kong, or declare Mahjong.
  • You may declare concealed kongs and add to your pungs.

Robots discard to keep their hand as few tiles from ready as possible, counting the tiles still unseen that would improve it. When another player looks close to winning and their own hand is far from ready, they play safe, starting with tiles that player threw away. The Hint shows the same counts for your discard, and highlights the tile.

Each fan doubles the payment, so robots also weigh what each hand would score. They aim for a plan, such as Pure One Suit or Seven Pairs, counting only tiles outside their void suit, and give up a plan worth more when it is too many tiles away. The Hint names the plan and what it would score. Seven Pairs can’t claim discards, so robots count it for less the further it is from ready. A run can't be made from a discard either, but over 2,000 test hands counting runs for less played no better, so robots count them like pungs. Robots choose their void suit the same way, and the Hint button on that choice names the suit they would pick.

Scoring

Every win is worth at least 1 point, and each fan doubles the payment. Patterns that add fan: Pure One Suit (+2), All Pungs (+1), Seven Pairs (+2), Self-Drawn (+1), each Kong (+1), each set of four identical tiles in hand (+1), Last Tile (+1) and Win on Kong (+1). Payment is 2 to the power of the fan (capped at 8 fan, or at the Fan Cap the host chooses): a discard win is paid by the discarder, a self-drawn win by all three opponents. With After a Win set to Hand Ends, the hand ends at the first win.

Table Options

The host chooses these in Settings, and every phone at the table shows them under the wall count: the ones that are on, and any other choice that is not the usual one. Exchange Three, Blood Battle, Settling a Draw and Wind and Rain are on unless the host changes them.

  • Exchange Three: after the deal, each player chooses three tiles of one suit and passes them: to the next player in the first hand, to the player across in the second, to the player before in the third, and so on in turn. Then everyone chooses a void suit.
  • After a Win: Blood Battle (usual): a win does not end the hand. The winner's hand is turned up and they stop playing; the others play on until three players have won or the wall runs out. More than one player can win on the same discard, and a self-drawn win is paid only by the players still playing. The first to win deals the next hand. Blood Flow: no one stops after a win: everyone plays on until the wall runs out, and a player may win again and again. A winner's hand stays turned up as it won: they only win again or discard the tile they drew, and they still pay for the others' wins. The first to win deals the next hand. Hand Ends: the first win ends the hand.
  • Settling a Draw: when the wall runs out, each player who has not won and whose hand is not ready pays each ready player who has not won what that player's hand could win on a discard, at its best.
  • Wind and Rain: kongs are paid when they are made: a kong of a discard, 2 points by the discarder; a pung made into a kong, 1 point by each player still playing; a concealed kong, 2 points by each. Each kong still adds a fan. When the wall runs out, a player who has not won and whose hand is not ready pays back what their kongs got.
  • Self-Drawn Win: +1 fan (usual), or +1 point: a self-drawn win then adds no fan, and each player pays one point more.
  • Fan Cap: the most fan a hand can score: 3, 4, 8 (usual) or none.

Robots pass three tiles of their shortest suit, the one they will most likely void, choosing those with the fewest pairs and neighbors; the Hint highlights them. Over 6,000 test hands this played as well as trying every choice against likely tiles coming back. After Exchange Three, hands come together faster, so robots keep playing against a player close to winning until their own hand is three tiles from ready, rather than two. Robots weigh what a hand is worth under the cap in force, so with a low cap they chase big hands less. Robots declare every kong their hand plan allows: the extra fan is already worth more than the payment, and over 3,000 test hands counting the Wind and Rain payment as well changed no decision; the Hint shows what a kong collects. In Blood Flow a winner's hand stays turned up, so you can see the tiles it wins on; over 9,000 test hands, holding those tiles back played no better than ignoring them (a tile held back slows your own hand, and the winner can still win off the wall), so robots play their own hand, counting the turned-up tiles as seen.
