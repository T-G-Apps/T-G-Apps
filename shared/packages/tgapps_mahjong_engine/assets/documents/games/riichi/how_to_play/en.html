How to Play Riichi Mahjong

Japanese Mahjong with yaku, riichi and dora.

The Goal

Complete a hand of four sets and a pair (or seven pairs, or the Thirteen Orphans) that contains at least one yaku (scoring pattern). Everyone starts with 25,000 points; the highest score after the chosen number of hands wins.

Setup

136 tiles (no flowers). The wall shows a dora indicator: the next tile in sequence is a dora and adds one han to a winning hand for each copy you hold. Each kong turns up another indicator (kan dora). When a player who declared Riichi wins, the tile under each indicator is turned up too: these ura dora add han the same way, for that winner only.

Playing

  • On your turn you draw a tile, then discard one. Tap a tile once to lift it and tap it again to discard.
  • When another player discards, you may claim the tile: Chow (a run of three in one suit, only from the player before you), Pung (three identical tiles) or Kong (four identical tiles, which also gives you a replacement tile). A win beats a pung/kong, and a pung/kong beats a chow.
  • You can also declare a concealed kong from four identical tiles in your hand, or add a fourth tile to one of your pungs.
  • Riichi: with a fully concealed hand that is one tile from winning, tap Riichi and discard one of the highlighted tiles. You pay 1,000 points into the pot and cannot change your hand afterwards, but Riichi is a yaku and the pot goes to the winner.

Robots discard to keep their hand as few tiles from ready as possible, counting the tiles still unseen that would improve it. When another player looks close to winning and their own hand is far from ready, they play safe, starting with tiles that player threw away. The Hint shows the same counts for your discard, and highlights the tile.

A hand needs a yaku to win and is paid more for each han, so robots also weigh what each hand would score. They aim for a plan, such as Tanyao, Honitsu or Chiitoitsu, counting the dora it keeps, and give up a plan worth more when it is too many tiles away. A concealed hand has Riichi as its yaku, so robots claim a discard only for a plan with a yaku of its own. The Hint names the plan and what it would score. Chiitoitsu can’t claim discards, so robots count it for less the further it is from ready.

Yaku Included

Riichi, Menzen Tsumo, Tanyao, Yakuhai (dragons, seat wind, prevailing wind), Pinfu, Iipeiko, Chiitoitsu, Toitoi, Sanshoku Doujun, Ittsu, Honitsu, Chinitsu, Sanankou, Honroutou, Shousangen, Haitei/Houtei, Rinshan Kaihou and dora. Yakuman: Kokushi Musou, Daisangen, Suuankou and Tsuuiisou.

Scoring

Han and fu decide the payment. Fu start at 20. Winning on a discard with a concealed hand adds 10, and a self-drawn win adds 2. Each pung adds 2 for simples or 4 for terminals and honors, twice as much if concealed, and four times as much for a kong. A pair of dragons, or of your seat wind or the prevailing wind, adds 2 (4 when it is both winds). A winning tile that completes the pair, fills the middle of a run, or is the 3 to a 1-2 (or the 7 to an 8-9) adds 2. The fu are rounded up to the next 10. Pinfu is 20 fu self-drawn and 30 on a discard, an open hand with nothing more is 30, and seven pairs is 25. The win summary shows the han and the fu. Mangan comes at 5 han, or at 4 han with 40 fu or 3 han with 70; haneman at 6 han, baiman at 8, sanbaiman at 11 and yakuman at 13. Robots still value a plan at 30 fu: valuing it at the fu it would likely score played worse over 8,000 test hands. A hand may not win on a tile it has previously discarded (furiten).

The dealer deals again after winning, or when the wall runs out with the dealer's hand ready; otherwise the deal passes to the next player. Each time the dealer deals again, and after the wall runs out, a honba (repeat counter) is added, shown beside the pot: each adds 300 points to the next win, paid by the discarder, or 100 by each player on a self-drawn win. A win by a player other than the dealer clears them. Robots count the pot and the honba in what each plan would win: over 16,000 test hands that did a little better.

Table Options

The host chooses these in Settings, and every phone at the table shows the ones that are on, under the wall count. All five are on unless the host turns them off.

  • Open Tanyao: Tanyao counts in a hand with claimed sets (kuitan). Off: only in a closed hand.
  • Kuikae Ban: after a chow or pung, you may not discard the tile you claimed, or the tile at the other end of your chow. For example, after claiming a 4 onto 5-6, you may not discard a 4 or a 7.
  • Double Ron: everyone who can win on a discard wins, and the discarder pays each of them; the riichi pot and the honba go to the first in turn after the discarder. Off: only that player wins. If the dealer is among the winners, the dealer deals again.
  • Bust Ending: the game ends when a player's score falls below zero.
  • Red Fives: one five of each suit is red, and counts as a dora: one han for each you hold. When you hold a red five and a plain one, tap the one you want to discard; robots keep the red one.
  • Not-Ready Payments: When the wall runs out, players whose hands are not ready pay 3,000 points in all to those whose hands are ready, unless all or none are ready.
